What the review examines

Claim, evidence, mechanism, and decision risk.

Every review applies the same core tests. Larger engagements cover more claims, more evidence, and more uncertainty.

Claim architecture

What is asserted, what is implied, which assumptions must be true, and whether the language outruns the evidence.

Methods and inference

Study design, measurement, sampling, statistical reasoning, causal interpretation, robustness, replicability, and generalizability.

Decision implications

What can be concluded now, what remains uncertain, which risks matter most, and what evidence would change the decision.

My work spans cognitive neuroscience, clinical neuroimaging, aging, evidence synthesis, measurement, EEG and ERP, and applied human factors. That breadth helps identify whether a claim fails at the level of study design, measurement, mechanism, interpretation, or real-world transfer.

Good fit

Questions that benefit from independent scientific scrutiny.

  • Does the evidence support the proposed mechanism or use case?
  • Is a result statistically real but practically unimportant?
  • What assumptions are concealed by persuasive technical language?
  • Would the findings generalize beyond the study population or setting?
  • What evidence would most efficiently reduce uncertainty?
Boundaries

What this service does not provide.

  • Promotional endorsement or a predetermined conclusion
  • Legal, investment, medical, clinical, or regulatory advice
  • Ghostwritten scientific authority for unsupported claims
  • Primary data collection, laboratory testing, or product-validation studies
  • Outcome guarantees
